We fund llama and alpaca research at Oregon State University.

The North West Camelid Foundation began in 1986 with owners and breeders who wanted better veterinary care for their animals. The money we raise pays for studies at OSU's Carlson College of Veterinary Medicine, keeps a herd at the university for researchers and students, and funds a yearly scholarship.

What the money has paid for

From 1987 through 2024 the foundation put $700,521 into camelid research. Of that, $553,954 went to individual studies and $146,567 went to feeding and housing the llamas and alpacas that OSU researchers and students work with.

The early grants looked at uterine torsion, rickets and vitamin D, and how llamas process common drugs. These are the most recent:

"I was contacted by the University of Georgia about a sick cria. They tried the dose rate from our NWCF study and saw a fourfold increase in white count in 48 hours."

Dr. Erica McKenzie, large animal medicine, Oregon State University

The scholarship

Every year since 1993 the foundation has given a scholarship to a veterinary student with an interest in llamas and alpacas. In 2002 it became a permanent endowment at OSU, and the college's own committee now picks the recipient. Awards so far come to more than $44,000.

Why medical researchers want llama antibodies

In 1989 scientists found that llamas, alpacas and other camelids make an antibody that is missing a piece every other mammal's antibodies have. In the lab it can be cut down further into a nanobody, which is small enough to reach places ordinary antibodies can't.

At OSU, Dr. Chris Cebra and his colleagues are making nanobodies aimed at tuberculosis, dengue virus, and cancer proteins in dogs. The foundation's 2022 grant paid for one of these antibody projects.

Where we get together

Most of what we raise comes from one fundraiser a year, usually on a member's farm, with lunch, a silent auction, and an update from the OSU vets. We also hold Meet & Greets for owners and co-sponsor a camelid medicine conference at OSU.
